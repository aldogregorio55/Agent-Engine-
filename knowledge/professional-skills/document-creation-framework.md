# Document Architecture - Quick Reference

**The Problem** - You hold context the reader doesn't. The document must transfer your knowledge to someone entering cold, with minimal friction, in the right order.

---

## Before Writing

1. Who is reading this? (Assume senior management, but portable to anyone)
2. What is the main finding or core thesis?
3. What needs to be understood first before anything else makes sense?

---

## Structure Rules (Minto)

- Lead with the main finding or thesis. First thing they read is the answer
- Group supporting points logically. Concepts should flow into each other - if the reader has to jump back to understand something, the order is wrong
- Each section answers "why?" or "how?" for the point above it

---

## Self Test

Read only the first sentence of each section. If those sentences don't tell the complete story in the right order, restructure.

---

## Final Step

Before sending out: paste into a clean window. Read it cold. Does it stand alone? Would the first paragraph give a senior manager the core thesis in 30 seconds?

---

## What To Cut

If you didn't discuss it, it doesn't belong. If it doesn't serve the thesis, it doesn't belong.

---

# AI Behavioral Instructions

When producing documents for external readers:

- Lead with the main finding or core thesis. Never build towards a conclusion
- Assume the reader has zero prior context. The document must stand alone
- Front-load what needs to be understood first. Order by logical dependency, not by how the thinking developed
- Group related concepts together. The reader should never need to jump back
- First sentence of each section carries the core point of that section
- Optimize for senior stakeholder scanning in 30 seconds
- Stay lean. Cut anything that doesn't directly support the thesis
- Before finalizing, pressure-test: what would a reader need to understand first, and is that what they see first?
