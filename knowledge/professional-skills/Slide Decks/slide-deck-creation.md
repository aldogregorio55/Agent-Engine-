# Slide Deck Creation Principles

Companion to [`slide-deck-production-pipeline.md`](./slide-deck-production-pipeline.md): this file is the **craft** (what makes a good slide). That file is the **process** (how to produce a deck with AI).

**The Problem** — Slides are not documents. A document has a reader who can re-read and scan. A slide has a viewer who glances and a listener who hears you speak over it. Different medium, different rules.

---

## Principles

**1. One idea per section. State the purpose, not the activity.**

"To [deliver X] that [achieves Y]" — not "We did A, B, and C."

If your section has three bullets that are really one idea fragmented, you have one idea with supporting evidence underneath it. Recognise the difference.

---

**2. Face forward.**

Slides are about what something means — not what you did to get here. Past tense is for findings docs. Slides answer "so what?" and "what now?"

---

**3. Be specific or say nothing.**

Name the platform. Name the attack type. Name the model. Vague language ("our systems", "various techniques", "key stakeholders") adds words without adding information. If it could appear in any deck from any team, it's filler.

---

**4. Define what good looks like inside the claim.**

Don't just state what you're doing — state what success and failure look like. If the reader can't evaluate whether your solution worked from the slide alone, the slide is incomplete.

---

**5. Conditional > unconditional.**

"If X is validated, then Y" signals rigour. Unconditional promises in uncertain work sound either naive or dishonest. State what depends on what.

---

**6. Match the altitude to the audience.**

Implementation detail belongs in working docs. Slides carry outcomes and observable claims. If someone who's never touched your tools can't parse the bullet, it's too low.

---

**7. One line carries the weight.**

If every bullet is formatted the same, the audience decides what matters — you've lost control. Emphasise one takeaway per section. The rest supports it.

---

**8. The slide is what they'll remember. You are what they'll hear.**

Never write on a slide what you'll say out loud. The slide is the photograph they take home. The story, the transitions, the "where we started" — that's verbal. Overlap between the two means one of them is redundant.

---

## Self-Test

Read only your section headings and first lines. No bullets. Does it tell a coherent story? If not, restructure until it does.
